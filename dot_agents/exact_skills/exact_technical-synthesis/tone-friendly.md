# Tone: Friendly

The friendly tone elaborates more, varies rhythm more, and welcomes the reader in. It's the right register for a public blog, internal docs aimed at a broad audience, or a deliverable to a client whose culture leans warm. The work is still substantive — friendly relaxes register, not rigor.

The friendly tone is *not* casual, breezy, or chummy. AI-tell hygiene from `voice-and-flow.md` still applies in full, and every always-standard from `SKILL.md` still holds. The shift from expert tone is in elaboration, transitions, and a slightly warmer relationship with the reader — not in dropping standards.

## Register

- **Elaborated.** A point that gets one tight sentence in expert tone gets a sentence plus a clarifying example in friendly tone. The elaboration earns its space by adding signal — never by padding.
- **Warmer.** The reader is a colleague being walked through something, not a target receiving signal. Direct address is permitted.
- **First or second person permitted** when load-bearing. "We chose X because Y" is fine; "I noticed Z when investigating W" is fine; "you'll want to set Q before running R" is fine. Used in moderation, not as a verbal tic.

## Rhythm and length

- **Varied sentence length.** Long and short alternate. A long sentence with a worked example sits next to a short one stating the point.
- **More transitions than expert tone, still tight.** A sentence connecting two sections is fine; a paragraph orienting the reader to what comes next is not.
- **Paragraphs can elaborate.** Two or three sentences expanding a single idea is normal, where expert tone would have one tight sentence. Three becomes a smell — the third sentence usually wants to be a separate bullet or a cut.

## Allowed devices

- **Mild metaphor** when illuminating. "BM25 is the unfashionable lexical baseline" is metaphor doing work. "BM25 is the trusty workhorse of search" is metaphor as cliche; cut it.
- **Worked examples and brief asides** when they add signal. Real ones, with real names.
- **Second person** in moderation. Not in every paragraph.
- **First person plural ("we")** when it includes the reader in a shared step. "We can collapse those three sections into one" is fine; "we'll explore three approaches" is throat-clearing.
- **Direct questions to the reader** sparingly, only when the question is genuine. Rhetorical questions with immediate answers are a `voice-and-flow.md` violation.

## Tone-specific anti-patterns

- "Real talk" register — "Look, here's the thing..."
- Chummy intimacy — "We've all been there", "you know how it is", "let's be honest"
- Sass or knowing winks — "yes, really", "spoiler alert", "fun fact"
- Casual filler that doesn't carry meaning — "kinda", "sort of", "basically", "honestly"
- Excessive second-person — every paragraph addressing the reader directly
- Metaphor as decoration — "the data flows like a river through the pipeline"
- Treating friendliness as license to drop standards — recaps, primers, doc-site-reflex symptoms are still cut
